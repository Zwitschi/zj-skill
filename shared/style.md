# Style rules

Style is subordinate to evidence. Never change a fact to improve the phrasing.

## General

- Write in one output language throughout, unless the user asked for bilingual
  documents.
- Keep product and technology names unchanged.
- Prefer concrete nouns and strong verbs over adjectives and claims.
- Remove filler and generic enthusiasm. Say what was done and what changed.
- Do not use em dashes. Use commas, colons, or a new sentence. Use an en dash
  only between dates.
- Apply the configured spelling convention consistently.

## CV bullets

- Start with a strong verb.
- Use past tense for past roles and present tense for the current role.
- One idea per bullet.
- Put the result first when there is one: what was done, how, with what effect.
- Quantify only with approved figures and keep the confidence tag in the working
  copy.
- Do not use subheadings inside a role.
- Do not repeat the same achievement in several bullets.

## Contribution paragraph

- First person, prose, not bullets.
- About six lines.
- Open with a variation of "with X years of experience in ...".
- Then answer, in order: who I am, what I do, how I do it, what results I
  achieve.
- Use two or three of the strongest verified results.
- Do not repeat the profile or list education.

## Profile bullets

- Three to six bullets.
- Each bullet is a compressed positioning statement.
- Cover education, role identity, years of experience, and named Green
  technologies.
- Do not include results or value claims; those belong in the contribution.

## Experience integration

- Never change the official job title.
- Adapt at most one bullet per role.
- Use a Green term only where the underlying task is real and verifiable.
- If no genuine overlap exists, do not force one.
- Do not upgrade the seniority implied by the original bullet.

## Cover letter

- Explain why this role fits the career path, the underlying motivation, and how
  the development leads to the role.
- Use Yellow terms as the narrative thread.
- Use Green and Red terms sparingly, in support.
- Do not use filler openings, generic praise of the company, or clichés.
- Do not repeat the CV.
- Do not claim a connection to the employer that is not evidenced.

## Dates and numbers

- Use month/year for roles and years for education, taken from official
  documents.
- Keep numbers exactly as sourced; mark estimates as estimates.
- Do not round a figure without saying so.

## Final documents

- Remove working tags only when the user asks for a submission copy.
- Keep open items visible in the working copy.
- Label every generated document as a draft until the user approves it.
