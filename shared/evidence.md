# Evidence and fact discipline

Every claim in a CV, cover letter, fit report, or change log must trace to a
source or to an explicit candidate answer. This rule overrides convenience,
style, and scoring.

## No invention

Never invent or infer:

- skills, tools, technologies, or certifications;
- employers, clients, job titles, or dates;
- team sizes, budgets, volumes, percentages, durations, or other metrics;
- outcomes, responsibilities, or achievements;
- degrees, grades, permits, or language levels.

If the evidence is missing, say so. A gap named honestly is better than a claim
that cannot be defended in an interview.

## Source tags

Tag every factual item with where it came from. Default vocabulary:

| Tag                 | Meaning                                                                                      |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `[VERIFIED]`        | Confirmed by a reference letter, employment confirmation, or equivalent third-party document |
| `[CREDENTIAL]`      | Confirmed by a diploma, transcript, certificate, or equivalent                               |
| `[SELF]`            | Stated only in the candidate's own materials, such as a CV, cover letter, or profile         |
| `[VERIFIED / SELF]` | Stated in both a third-party document and the candidate's own materials                      |

If the user has an existing tag scheme, map the default tags to it and keep the
meaning. Tags stay in the working documents and are removed from final
submission copies only when the skill explicitly says so.

## Confidence

For any number, date, or outcome collected from the candidate, record how
certain it is:

- exact;
- estimated;
- approximate.

Later documents must not state an estimate as if it were exact.

## Conflicts

When sources disagree:

1. The official or third-party document wins over the candidate's own CV.
2. Flag the difference and where it appears.
3. Do not silently pick a value.
4. If the conflict cannot be resolved, list it as an open item.

When the same achievement appears in several versions:

- keep the most specific wording, including numbers;
- merge the rest without losing a unique fact;
- keep facts that appear in only one version, because those are the ones later
  rewrites tend to drop.

## Gaps

- Name missing evidence explicitly. Example: "Term X is a specific requirement
  in the posting but is not evidenced in the candidate's documents."
- Do not fill a gap with a plausible guess, a synonym, or a related skill the
  candidate has not demonstrated.
- A gap can be a legitimate reason not to apply. Say so.

## Open items

Anything that needs confirmation is written as a short bracketed note where it
applies, for example `[confirm: exact date]` or `[confirm: team size]`. Open
items are collected in the verification or handoff output and removed only when
resolved.

## Skill levels

When rating a skill:

- use a 1 to 5 scale with a written definition for each level;
- require at least one piece of evidence for a high level;
- mark a level taken only from the candidate's own materials with an asterisk;
- rate conservatively;
- keep the evidence next to the level.

## Reference letters

- Evaluative phrases such as "to our full satisfaction" are evidence for the
  coach, not CV content.
- Positive descriptions of working style may be kept as quotes in a selection
  pool, attributed to the employer.
- Never turn a reference letter into a claim the letter does not support.

## Job titles

The official title from the employment document or reference letter stays
unchanged. Alternative titles may be kept as headline options, never substituted
silently into the experience section.
